Self-watering planters are the most useful upgrade for a sunny balcony, and the easiest to get wrong. The marketing all sounds the same, so here is what actually separates a good one from a soggy mess.

## The short answer

If you only want one thing: pick a planter with a reservoir big enough to cover your longest absence, an overflow hole, and a fill tube you can reach without moving the plant.

::product{id="sample-self-watering-planter"}

## What matters

### Reservoir size

The reservoir sets how long you can leave it. Match it to your real routine, not the best case: a weekend away is a very different ask from a fortnight.

### Weight when full

Balconies have limits. Check the full weight of planter, soil and a full reservoir, not just the empty planter weight on the listing.

::figure{src="/images/placeholder/photo-landscape.svg" alt="Placeholder photo of two planters side by side on a balcony floor" caption="PLACEHOLDER — a side-by-side photo comparing two planters, with a caption saying what to notice."}

### Overflow and drainage

Heavy rain on an open balcony can swamp a reservoir. An overflow hole is not optional, and it should drain somewhere that will not annoy the neighbour below.

## Who should skip them

If you grow mostly drought-tolerant herbs, or you are home every day and enjoy the watering ritual, a plain pot is cheaper and lighter. Self-watering planters earn their cost on thirsty crops like tomatoes and salad in summer.
